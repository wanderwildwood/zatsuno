title: Hands-only CPR
keywords: cardiac arrest, collapse, collapsed, not breathing, chest compressions, AED, defibrillator, heart attack, resuscitation, rescue breaths, CPR, child, baby, drowning
source: NHLBI (NIH), "Cardiac Arrest: Treatment"; MedlinePlus (NIH), "CPR: adult and child after onset of puberty"; American Heart Association, 2025 Guidelines for CPR and ECC, Heartsaver high-quality CPR summary; Wilderness Medical Society, "CPR in the Wilderness" guideline summary; FCC, "911 Wireless Services".

! Collapsed, not responding to a shout or tap, and not breathing normally (gasping is not normal). Adult, child or baby.
1. Call 911 or your local emergency number, or have someone call, on speaker. No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, use a satellite messenger or send others. Alone with no way to call: start CPR.
2. Send someone for an AED (defibrillator) if one might be nearby.
3. On their back on firm, flat ground. Heel of one hand on the lower half of the breastbone, center of the chest, other hand on top.
4. Push hard and fast: at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep, 100 to 120 a minute (under two a second).
- Child: one or two hands, about 2 inches (5 cm). Baby: two thumbs or the heel of one hand on the breastbone just below the nipples, about 1.5 inches (4 cm).
5. Let the chest come all the way back up each time.
6. Don't stop until help takes over or they breathe normally. Pause only when an AED says to. Breathing normally: put them on their side, keep them warm and watched. They still need a hospital.
# AED
- Turn it on and do what it says. Keep pushing while the pads go on.
- No one touches them while it checks or shocks. Then push again at once.
+++
# Rescue breaths
- Trained: 30 pushes, 2 breaths, repeat.
- Children, babies, drowning and lightning victims need breaths most. If you can't give them, push anyway.
> Hands-only CPR is current advice for an untrained bystander helping an adult (NHLBI, MedlinePlus).
- Two rescuers: swap every 2 minutes; tired arms push too shallow.
# Far from help
- No AED and no sign of life after 20 minutes: it is reasonable to stop (10 if a major injury, like a bad fall, caused it). Keep going longer if they are very cold or were hit by lightning.
