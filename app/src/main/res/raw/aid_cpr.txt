title: Hands-only CPR
keywords: cardiac arrest, collapse, collapsed, not breathing, chest compressions, AED, defibrillator, heart attack, resuscitation, rescue breaths, CPR
source: NHLBI (NIH), "Cardiac Arrest: Treatment"; MedlinePlus (NIH), "CPR: adult and child after onset of puberty".
---
! For an adult or teen who has collapsed and is not breathing normally.
1. Call 911 or your local emergency number, or have someone else call. Put the phone on speaker.
2. Send someone for an AED (defibrillator) if there might be one nearby.
3. Kneel beside them. Put the heel of one hand on the center of the chest, between the nipples, and your other hand on top.
4. Push hard and fast: about 2 inches (5 cm) deep, 100 to 120 times a minute. That is a little under two a second.
5. Let the chest come all the way back up after each push.
6. Do not stop until help takes over, an AED tells you to, or the person starts breathing normally.
# AED
- Turn it on and do what it says. Keep pushing while it gets ready.
# If you are trained
- Give 30 compressions, then 2 rescue breaths, and repeat.
- For a child, a baby, or someone pulled from water, breaths matter more. Give them if you know how.
> Hands-only CPR is the current advice for an untrained bystander helping an adult (NHLBI, MedlinePlus). Older advice started every round with breaths.
- If someone else is there, swap every 2 minutes. Tired arms push too shallow.
