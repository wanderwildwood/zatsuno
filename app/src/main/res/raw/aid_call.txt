# Call
1. Call 911, or your local emergency number. In Europe and many other countries 112 works from any mobile phone.
2. Put the phone on speaker so your hands are free.
3. Stay on the line until the call-taker tells you to hang up.
# What to tell them
1. Where you are. Read out the position above, and how close it is (the ± figure).
2. What you can see: the trail, the trailhead you started from, a lake, a ridge, a road.
3. The number you are calling from.
4. What happened, and how many people are hurt.
5. How each person is: awake or not, breathing or not, bleeding or not.
- Many call-takers can talk you through CPR or bleeding control. Do what they ask.
# No signal
- Move to higher ground or open sky and try again. A call can get through where data cannot.
- In many parts of the US you can text 911. Call if you can, text if you can't. Put your position in the first text.
- Three of anything is a call for help: three whistle blasts, three shouts, three flashes.
- If you are lost, stay where you are. You are easier to find standing still.
