# Call
1. Call 911 or your local emergency number (112 in Europe and many other countries).
2. Put the phone on speaker. Stay on the line until told to hang up. If the call drops, call back.
3. After the call, keep the phone on for rescuers. Use it for nothing else.
# What to tell them
1. Where you are: read out the position above and its ± figure.
2. What you can see: trail, trailhead, lake, ridge or road.
3. The number you are calling from.
4. What happened, how many are hurt, and whether each is awake, breathing and bleeding.
- The call-taker may talk you through CPR or bleeding control. Do what they ask.
# No signal
- Satellite messenger, PLB or phone satellite SOS: use it now.
- Try 911 even with no service showing: any carrier's tower must carry it.
- Still failing: if safe, try from higher ground or open sky nearby. A call can get through where data cannot.
- Can't call? Text 911 (many US areas take it) or someone who can call for you. Position first.
- Signal in threes: three whistle blasts, shouts or flashes.
- Still nothing: send two people out to call if you can, with the position and what happened written down. The rest stay with the injured.
- Alone with them: make them as safe as you can, then go. Not if you are doing CPR or they are choking: stay and keep going.
- Lost: stay put unless the spot is unsafe (rising water, rockfall, fire). You are easier to find standing still.
