! Can cough hard, speak or cry: let them keep coughing, and stay with them. Don't reach into an awake person's mouth.
# Can't speak, cough or breathe: adult or child over 1
1. Have someone call 911 or your local emergency number. Alone: shout for help and start now. No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, keep going.
2. Stand behind them, arm across their chest, and lean them forward.
3. Give 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
4. Then 5 abdominal thrusts: fist just above the navel, other hand over it, pull sharply in and up.
5. Keep going, 5 and 5, until the object comes out or they collapse.
- Pregnant, or too big to reach around: arms around the chest, fist on the middle of the breastbone, thrust straight back.
- Choking alone: your own fist in and up above your navel, or push your upper belly hard against a chair back or railing.
# Baby under 1
- Call for help as above and start now. Never give a baby abdominal thrusts.
1. Baby face down along your forearm, head lower than the body, jaw held in your fingers.
2. Give 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades.
3. Turn face up. Give 5 chest thrusts: heel of one hand mid-breastbone, just below the nipples.
4. Repeat until the object comes out or the baby stops responding.
# If they collapse or stop responding
1. Lay them on a firm surface and start CPR (see Hands-only CPR). Compressions can push the object out. No one has called? Shout for help. Alone with a baby: call on speaker; if you must leave to call, give 2 minutes of CPR first.
2. Before any breath, look in the mouth. Remove the object only if you can see it and it is loose. Never sweep blindly or grab at anything in the throat.

> Changed: adults now get back blows as well as thrusts, and a baby gets the heel of one hand, not two fingers.
- See a doctor after any thrusts. Get help now for a cough that won't stop, wheezing, short breath, trouble swallowing or speaking, or after CPR.
