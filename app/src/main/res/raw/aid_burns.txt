title: Burns

source: MedlinePlus (NIH), "Burns", "Electrical injury" and "Chemical burn or reaction"; American Red Cross Scientific Advisory Council, "Burn Cooling" (2019); American Heart Association and American Red Cross, "Guidelines for First Aid" (2024).
---
! Call 911 or your local emergency number for burns bigger than their palm, deep, charred or white, on the face, hands, feet, groin or a joint, all the way around a limb or the chest, or from electricity or chemicals.
! Singed nose hair or eyebrows, soot in the mouth or nose, hoarse voice or cough after smoke or flames: the airway can swell shut over hours. Get help now.
! No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, head out now.
# On fire
- Stop, drop and roll, or smother the flames with a coat or blanket, then pour on water.
# Do this
1. Get them away from the heat. Electricity: power off first, and stay 20 feet (6 m) from power lines until they're off. Can't turn it off: push them free with something dry, not metal. Not breathing normally: see Hands-only CPR.
2. Chemicals: brush off powder, take off soaked clothing and jewelry, rinse with running water for 15 minutes or more.
3. Cool with cool, clean water, not ice, for at least 10 minutes, 20 if you can, even up to 3 hours later. Keep the rest of them warm.
4. Take off rings, watches and tight things before it swells. Leave clothing stuck to the skin.
5. Cover loosely with a clean, dry, non-stick dressing or cloth.
6. Large burn: watch for shock. See Shock.
+++
7. Small burn: keep it clean and covered. Pus, fever, red streaks, worse pain or pain after 2 days: get help; far out, head out now.
# Don't
- No butter, oil, ice, toothpaste or home remedies.
- Don't break blisters.
- Nothing on a serious burn but a clean cover.
