title: Severe bleeding
keywords: blood, bleeding, hemorrhage, tourniquet, pressure, wound, cut, gash, windlass, amputation
source: DHS, "Stop the Bleed: Applying a Tourniquet"; MedlinePlus (NIH), "Bleeding" and "Cuts and puncture wounds".
---
! Call 911 or your local emergency number. Someone can bleed to death in a few minutes.
# Press
1. Gloves on if you have them. Find where the blood is coming from; cut or tear clothing away.
2. Press hard on the wound with a clean cloth, gauze, or your hand. Lean your weight into it.
3. If blood soaks through, put more cloth on top and keep pressing. Do not lift it to look.
4. A deep wound in the armpit, neck or groin: pack cloth into it, then press.
# Tourniquet, for an arm or leg
- Use one when pressure does not stop the bleeding, or when you can't keep pressing.
1. Put it 2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) above the wound, between the wound and the heart. Over clothing is fine.
2. Not on a joint: not on the elbow, knee, wrist or ankle.
3. Pull the strap tight, then twist the rod until the bleeding stops. It will hurt. That is expected.
4. Lock the rod so it can't unwind.
5. Write down the time it went on. Do not take it off.
- No tourniquet: keep pressing hard. A wide band of cloth twisted tight with a stick can work. A thin cord or wire cuts and does not.
> A tourniquet is now used early for life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg (DHS Stop the Bleed). Older advice kept it as a last resort.
# Then
- Keep them lying down and warm. See Shock.
