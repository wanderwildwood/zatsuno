title: Severe bleeding
keywords: blood, bleeding, hemorrhage, tourniquet, pressure, wound, cut, gash, windlass, amputation
source: DHS, "Stop the Bleed: Applying a Tourniquet"; MedlinePlus (NIH), "Bleeding" and "Cuts and puncture wounds"; AHA and American Red Cross, "2024 Guidelines for First Aid".
---
! Call 911 or your local emergency number. Someone can bleed to death in a few minutes.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, keep pressing and send someone.
# Press
1. Gloves on if you have them. Find where the blood comes from; cut or tear clothing away.
  Do not pull out anything stuck in the wound. Press around it.
2. Press hard on the wound with a clean cloth, gauze, or your hand. Lean your weight into it.
3. Blood soaks through: add more cloth on top and keep pressing. Do not lift it to look. Once it stops, tie the cloth on firmly.
4. Deep wound in the armpit, neck or groin: pack cloth into it, then press.
  Chest or belly wound: do not pack it. Cover it with cloth; do not seal it.
# Tourniquet, for an arm or leg
- Use one right away if blood spurts, pools or soaks through, or if pressing fails or you can't keep pressing. Keep pressing until it is on.
1. Put it 2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) above the wound, between the wound and the heart. Over clothing is fine.
2. Not on a joint (elbow, knee, wrist, ankle). Wound on or just below a joint: put it above the joint.
3. Pull the strap tight, then twist the rod until the bleeding stops. It will hurt; keep going.
4. Lock the rod so it can't unwind. Still bleeding? Put a second one just above the first.
5. Write down the time it went on. Do not loosen it or take it off.
- No tourniquet: keep pressing hard. A wide cloth band twisted tight with a stick can work; thin cord or wire cannot.
+++
> Tourniquets now go on early, not as a last resort (DHS Stop the Bleed).
# Then
- Keep them lying down and warm. See Shock.
- Tourniquet, packed wound, or chest or belly wound: they need a hospital. Get help or head out now.
