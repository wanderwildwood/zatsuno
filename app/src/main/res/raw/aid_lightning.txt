# Someone is struck
1. Call 911 or your local emergency number. No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, start care, then send someone for help.
2. It is safe to touch them. They carry no charge.
3. Lightning still close: move to a safer place.
4. Not breathing normally: start CPR, with rescue breaths if you can, and use an AED if there is one. See Hands-only CPR.
- Keep giving breaths until they breathe on their own. Breathing can stay stopped after the heart restarts.
5. Several people hurt: help first anyone not breathing. Someone moaning or moving is breathing.
6. Look for burns, broken bones and head injury. Keep them warm.
7. Anyone struck needs a doctor, even if they seem fine. Get them out or send for help.

# Staying safe
- When thunder roars, go indoors. No place outside is safe in a thunderstorm.
- A hard-topped car or a building with plumbing and wiring is safe. Tents, picnic shelters and caves are not.
- Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back out.
- Caught out: get off peaks and ridges, away from lone trees, water and metal. Do not lie flat. Spread a group at least 20 feet (6 m) apart.
> Crouching was once taught as safe. The National Weather Service no longer calls it safe; it only lowers the risk a little.
> The 30-30 rule said to take shelter when thunder came within 30 seconds of the flash. The National Weather Service has dropped that half: lightning reaches 10 to 12 miles out from a storm, farther than thunder carries, so thunder you can hear at all means you are in range. The other half stands: wait 30 minutes after the last thunder.
