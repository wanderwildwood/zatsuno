# Someone is struck
1. Call 911 or your local emergency number.
2. It is safe to touch them. A person struck by lightning carries no charge.
3. Move to a safer place if lightning is still close; the storm may still be overhead.
4. Not breathing normally: start CPR, and use an AED if there is one.
5. Several people hurt: help first anyone who is not breathing. Someone who is moaning or moving is breathing.
6. Look for burns, broken bones and head injury, and keep them warm.
# Staying safe
- When thunder roars, go indoors. No place outside is safe in a thunderstorm.
- A hard-topped car or a building with plumbing and wiring is safe. Tents, picnic shelters and caves are not.
- Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back out.
- Caught out: get off peaks and ridges, away from lone trees, water and metal. Spread a group out.
> Crouching low on the balls of the feet was once taught as a safe position. The National Weather Service no longer calls it safe; it only lowers the risk a little.
