! Out of the water, not responding and not breathing normally (or only gasping): start CPR now. Call 911 or your local emergency number, or have someone call.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone and keep going.
# Reach, throw, row, don't go
1. Reach with a pole, branch or rope. Stay low on shore.
2. Throw anything that floats.
3. Row out wearing a life jacket.
4. Don't go in unless trained in water rescue. Panicking swimmers drown rescuers.
- Through ice: don't walk out to them. Reach or throw from shore. If you must go onto the ice, lie flat to spread your weight.
- You fell through: turn back the way you came. Kick and pull onto the ice on your belly, then roll away. Don't stand up until you're on solid ice or land.
# Out of the water
1. Not breathing normally: trained, give 5 rescue breaths first, then repeat 30 pushes and 2 breaths. Can't give breaths: push anyway. See Hands-only CPR.
  Vomits: roll them on their side, clear the mouth, then keep going.
2. Breathing: roll them on their side so vomit can drain. Don't press the belly or try to get water out. Watch their breathing; start CPR if it stops.
- Dove into shallow water or hit something: keep the head and neck still if you can. Breathing and CPR still come first.
3. Remove wet clothes, dry and wrap them. See Hypothermia.
# Hospital
- Needed rescue breaths or CPR: they need a hospital now, even if they seem fine. Get help to come to them; don't let them hike out on their own.
- No symptoms, or only a mild cough that settles: they may stay out, but someone stays with them for 6 hours. Any of the signs below: go for help.
! Coughing that won't stop, short of breath, confused or very sleepy, now or hours later: call 911 or your local emergency number. Get help to come to them; don't let them hike out on their own.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone for help and stay with them.

# Cold water
- A sudden plunge causes gasping for about a minute. Float, head up, until breathing slows.
- Arms and legs weaken within about 10 minutes. Get out or hold something that floats.
> Breaths matter most here: drowning stops the heart from lack of air.
