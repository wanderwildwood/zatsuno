! Told to evacuate: go now. Follow what local officials say.
! Cut off by fire: get to a place with little or nothing to burn, such as a rock slide or a lake. Call your local emergency number (112 or 911).
  No signal: try 112 and 911 anyway (see Calling for help).
# Smoke
1. Stay inside. Staying inside is best.
2. Choose a room you can close off from outside air. Run a portable air cleaner or filter in it.
3. Don't burn anything indoors: candles, gas, propane, incense, wood. Don't smoke, fry or vacuum.
4. Central air: use a MERV 13 filter or better, and set it to recirculate.
5. Must go out: wear an N95 or P100 respirator marked NIOSH, sealed tight over nose and chin. Both straps on, the nose clip pressed firm.
- Check air quality at airnow.gov or on a phone weather app. Go out only when it is Good or Moderate.
! Asthma, COPD, heart disease, diabetes or pregnancy, and new or worse symptoms: get medical help. Trouble breathing: see Respiratory distress and asthma. Chest pain: see Chest pain.
+++
# What smoke does
- Coughing, wheezing, trouble breathing, stinging eyes, scratchy throat, runny nose, headache, tiredness, chest pain, fast heartbeat.
- Children, pregnant people, and anyone with asthma, COPD, heart disease, diabetes or kidney disease need the most care.
- Children 2 and older can wear a respirator; none come small enough for very young children. Choose one the child keeps on, over the nose and under the chin.
# Don't
- Don't count on cloth or surgical masks, bandanas (wet or dry) or tissues. They don't seal and don't filter smoke.
- A respirator filters smoke and ash only. It does not stop gases or give oxygen.
- Don't leave a home-made box-fan filter running unattended.
