title: Low blood sugar
keywords: low blood sugar, diabetes, diabetic, insulin, hypoglycemia, sugar low, shaky, sweaty, confused, acting drunk, passed out, glucose, juice, candy, glucagon
source: NIDDK (NIH), "Low Blood Glucose (Hypoglycemia)"; CDC, "Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycemia)"; MedlinePlus (NIH), "Low blood sugar - self-care"; AHA and American Red Cross, "2024 Guidelines for First Aid".
---
! Can't sit up, swallow safely or follow simple instructions: give nothing by mouth. Call 911 or your local emergency number.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone for help and stay with them.
# Signs
- Shaky, sweaty, pale, hungry, fast heartbeat.
- Confused, irritable, clumsy, slurred speech, acting strangely.
- Can look drunk or like a head injury. Check for a medical ID or a glucose meter.
- Not sure it's low sugar? If they're awake and can swallow, give sugar anyway.
- Never give their insulin: it makes low sugar worse.
# Awake and can swallow
1. Sit them down. Give sugar now: 4 glucose tablets or 1 tube of glucose gel. No tablets: half a cup (120 ml) of juice or regular (not diet) soda, 1 tablespoon of sugar or honey, or hard candies, jellybeans or gumdrops (check the label for about 15 grams of sugar). Not chocolate: fat slows the sugar.
2. Wait 15 minutes. Not better: give the same again.
3. Still not better, or getting worse: get help as above.
4. Once better, have them eat real food, like crackers, a sandwich or trail mix.
# Can't swallow
1. Nothing by mouth. Roll them onto their side to keep the airway clear. Put a pad or pack under them and cover them to keep them warm.
2. If they carry glucagon (nasal spray or pen) and you know how, give it as its label says. Call for help even if it works. They may vomit: keep them on their side.
3. Once awake and able to swallow, give sugar, then food.
4. They stop breathing normally: see Hands-only CPR.
+++
# Afterward
- Stay with them for at least an hour. Keep snacks handy; it can come back.
- After a hard day it can come back hours later, even overnight. Have them eat more and not hike alone.
- Head out and get them checked if they needed glucagon, weren't fully back to normal after sugar and food, or it keeps coming back.
  Back to normal, with their own food and supplies: they can usually carry on, eating often.
