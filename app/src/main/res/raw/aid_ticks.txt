# Remove it now
1. Grip the tick as close to the skin as you can with fine-tipped tweezers. Fingers if you have nothing else.
2. Pull straight out with steady, even pressure. Don't twist or jerk.
3. Mouthparts left in? Leave them. The skin pushes them out.
4. Clean the bite and your hands with soap and water, rubbing alcohol or sanitizer.
5. Seal the tick in a bag or wrap it in tape. Don't crush it with your fingers.
6. Check the whole body, hair and clothes for more.
# Afterwards
- Rash, fever, headache or body aches in the weeks after: see a doctor. Say when and where the bite happened.
! Fever and headache days after a bite, still on a trip: head out now. Some tick fevers can kill within days without treatment.
! Unsteady walking or leg weakness creeping upward, often in a child: check the scalp, hair and skin folds for a tick; remove it. Weakness reaches the arms or breathing: Call 911 or your local emergency number.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone for help.
+++
# Don't
- No petroleum jelly, nail polish, heat or matches. They waste time and can push the tick's fluids into the bite.
