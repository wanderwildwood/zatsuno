# Anaphylaxis: call 911 right away
- Trouble breathing, wheezing, a tight throat or a hoarse voice
- Swelling of the lips, tongue or face
- Hives spreading over the body, faintness, a weak fast pulse
- Can start within minutes of a sting, food or medicine
# Do this
1. Call 911 or your local emergency number.
2. If they carry epinephrine, help them use it now. An auto-injector goes into the outer thigh, through clothing if need be.
3. Lay them down with the feet raised. If breathing is hard, let them sit up instead.
4. If they are not getting better and there is a second injector, ask the call-taker about a second dose.
5. Stay with them. Start CPR if they stop breathing normally.
- Don't give anything by mouth if they are struggling to breathe.
> Epinephrine is the first treatment for anaphylaxis, used straight away (MedlinePlus). An antihistamine is not a substitute for it.
# A sting without those signs
1. Scrape the stinger out with a fingernail or the edge of a card. Don't squeeze it or use tweezers.
2. Wash with soap and water.
3. A cold pack on the sting helps the swelling.
- Watch them for the next hour. Signs of anaphylaxis can come on late.
