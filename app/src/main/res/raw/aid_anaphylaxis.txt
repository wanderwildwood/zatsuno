# Anaphylaxis: get help right away
- Trouble breathing, wheezing, a tight throat or a hoarse voice
- Swelling of the lips, tongue or face
- Hives spreading over the body, faintness, a weak fast pulse, vomiting or belly cramps
- Can start within minutes of a sting, food or medicine
# Do this
1. Call 911 or your local emergency number. No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, give epinephrine first, then send someone for help.
2. If they carry epinephrine, help them use it now: an auto-injector into the outer thigh, through clothing if need be, or a nasal spray up one nostril.
3. Lay them down with the feet raised (pregnant: on the left side). If breathing is hard, let them sit up instead. Don't let them stand or walk while signs remain.
4. No better 5 to 10 minutes after the first dose, or the signs come back: give the second dose if there is one.
5. Stay with them. Anyone with these signs needs a hospital, even if they get better: it can come back hours later.
6. Start CPR if they stop breathing normally (see Hands-only CPR).
- Nothing by mouth if they are struggling to breathe.
! Many stings at once, or a sting inside the mouth or throat: get medical help now, even without those signs.
+++
> Epinephrine is the first treatment, used straight away (MedlinePlus). An antihistamine is not a substitute for it.
# A sting without those signs
1. Move away from the insects. Scrape or pull the stinger out right away: speed matters more than method.
2. Wash with soap and water. A cold pack helps the swelling.
- Watch them for an hour. Signs can come on late.
