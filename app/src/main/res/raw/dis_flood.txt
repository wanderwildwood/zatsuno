! Water rising: get to higher ground now. Don't wait for an order to leave.
! Trapped by moving water: get to the highest point you can, then call your local emergency number (112 or 911).
  No signal: try 112 and 911 anyway (see Calling for help).
# Now
1. Get to higher ground. Told to evacuate: go at once. Lock up, and if there is time, switch off utilities and unplug appliances.
2. Turn around, don't drown. Never walk, wade or drive into floodwater, or drive around a barrier on a flooded road. The road under the water may be gone.
- 6 inches (15 cm) of moving water can knock an adult off their feet.
- 12 inches (30 cm) of moving water carries off most cars; 2 feet (60 cm) carries off SUVs and trucks.
3. Water over the outlets, cords under water, sparks, or buzzing, crackling or popping: get out of that room. Stay out of water that may carry electricity.
4. Keep listening to NOAA Weather Radio, local radio or TV.
+++
# After
- Stay out of standing water. It hides sewage, chemicals, sharp things, live wires and roads that have washed out.
- Don't go back into a flooded building until officials give the all clear. Floors can give way and ceilings fall. Have the power turned off before you go in.
- Drink only bottled, boiled or treated water until you are told the tap is safe. See Water disinfection.
- Throw out food that may have touched floodwater.
- A generator goes outside only, 20 feet (6 m) or more from doors, windows and vents. See Power outage.
- Obey road-closed signs. Let family know you are safe.
# Don't
- Don't drive into floodwater. Over half of flood drownings happen in a vehicle driven into it.
- Don't visit flooded areas to look. You get in the way of rescue.
