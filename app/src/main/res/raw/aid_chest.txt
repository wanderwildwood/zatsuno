title: Chest pain
keywords: chest pain, heart attack, angina, chest pressure, chest tightness, heart, cardiac, aspirin, arm pain, jaw pain, short of breath, indigestion
source: NHLBI (NIH), "Heart Attack: Symptoms"; CDC, "Heart Attack Symptoms, Risk, and Recovery"; MedlinePlus (NIH), "Chest pain"; AHA and American Red Cross, "2024 Guidelines for First Aid".
---
! Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or that comes back, may be a heart attack. Call 911 or your local emergency number now. Do not wait for it to pass.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone for help.
# Signs
- Pressure, squeezing or pain in the center of the chest.
- Pain spreading to an arm, the back, neck, jaw or upper belly.
- Short of breath, cold sweat, nausea, lightheaded.
- Women, older people and people with diabetes may have little chest pain: just breathlessness, nausea, back or jaw pain, or sudden tiredness.
# Do
1. Stop all effort. Sit them down, propped up however they breathe easiest.
2. Aspirin, if they are an adult, awake, the pain did not come from an injury, they are not allergic to it, have no bleeding problem, and no doctor told them to avoid it: chew 1 regular aspirin (325 mg) or 2 to 4 low-dose (81 mg).
3. Keep them warm and calm. Write down when the pain began and when you gave aspirin; send this with whoever goes for help.
4. If anyone might have an AED (defibrillator) nearby, send for it now.
5. They stop responding or breathing normally: start CPR and use the AED as soon as it arrives. See Hands-only CPR.
+++
# Getting out
- Do not let them walk out or carry a load, even if the pain eases.
- Do not let them drive themselves. Wait for the ambulance if one can come.
- Bring help to them. Carry them out only if no help can come.
