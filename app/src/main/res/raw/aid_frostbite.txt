# Signs
- Redness or pain first, then white or grayish-yellow skin
- Skin that feels unusually firm or waxy
- Numbness. Often someone else notices it first.
! Check for hypothermia too. It is the more dangerous of the two.
# Do this
1. Get them somewhere warm.
2. Don't walk on frostbitten feet or toes unless you have to.
3. Warm the area in warm water, not hot: comfortable to an unhurt hand.
4. No warm water: use body heat. Frozen fingers can go into an armpit.
5. Get medical care.
# Don't
- Don't rub it, or rub snow on it.
- Don't warm it by a fire, stove, heat lamp, heating pad or radiator. Numb skin burns easily.
- Don't thaw it if it could freeze again before you reach shelter. Refreezing does more harm.
