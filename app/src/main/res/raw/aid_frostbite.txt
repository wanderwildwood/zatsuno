# Signs
- Red or painful first, then white or grayish-yellow
- Firm or waxy skin
- Numbness. Often someone else notices it first.
! Check for hypothermia too and treat it first. See Hypothermia.
# Do this
1. Get them somewhere warm. Remove wet clothes, rings and anything tight before it swells.
2. Must walk out? Do it before thawing feet. Once thawed, don't walk on them: send for help.
3. Warm it in warm water, not hot: 98–102°F (37–39°C), comfortable to an unhurt hand. Keep it in until soft and colored, often 30 minutes. It will hurt.
4. No warm water: body heat, like fingers in an armpit.
5. Pad it loosely with dry cloth, between fingers and toes too. Keep it warm. Ibuprofen helps if they can take it.
6. Color and feeling back soon: likely frostnip. Protect it and carry on; watch for blisters for a day.
! Still numb, pale or blistered after warming: head out for medical care now. Some treatments only work within 24 hours.
+++
# Don't
- Don't rub it, or rub snow on it. Don't break blisters.
- No fire, stove, heat lamp, heating pad or radiator. Numb skin burns easily.
- Don't thaw it if it could refreeze before shelter. Refreezing does more harm.
