title: Poison ivy, oak and sumac

! Breathed smoke from these plants, or trouble breathing or a swollen face or throat after touching them: Call 911 or your local emergency number.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone for help.
# Do this
1. Wash the skin with soap and lots of cool water as soon as you can, best within 30 minutes. Rubbing alcohol works too. Scrub under the nails.
2. Rinse well so the oil doesn't spread. Wash hands before touching your face.
3. Get out of clothes and gloves that touched the plant, and keep them off bare skin until washed.
+++
# Know them
- Poison ivy: three glossy leaflets, a vine or low shrub. "Leaves of three, let it be."
- Poison oak: three fuzzy, lobed leaflets, a shrub, in the West and Southeast.
- Poison sumac: a tall shrub in swamps and wet ground, 7 to 13 smooth-edged leaflets in pairs.
# The oil (urushiol)
- It stays on clothes, boots, tools and pet fur for years. Wash clothes separately in hot water; wipe tools with alcohol or soapy water; bathe pets with pet shampoo, wearing gloves.
- The rash shows hours to days after contact, is worst on days 4 to 7, and lasts 1 to 3 weeks. It isn't catching; blister fluid doesn't spread it.
# The itch
- Cool, wet compresses or a cool soak.
- Calamine lotion or hydrocortisone cream from the kit, but not on broken skin or open blisters.
- An oral antihistamine they already use eases the itch.
- Try not to scratch, and leave blisters alone; open skin gets infected.
- No antihistamine cream on the rash; it can make the itch worse.
# Don't
- Don't burn the plants, even dead ones. The smoke carries the oil into lungs and eyes. Anyone who breathed the smoke needs emergency care, even before coughing or wheezing starts.
# Get medical care if
- The rash is on the face, eyes or genitals
- It covers more than a quarter of the body, or they can't sleep for itching
- Fever, pus, yellow crust, spreading redness or red streaks
- No better after 2 weeks
- They've had a severe reaction to these plants before: get care right away, don't wait for the rash
