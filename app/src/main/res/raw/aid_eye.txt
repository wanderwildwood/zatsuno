! Call 911 or your local emergency number for anything stuck in the eye, a cut eyeball, a chemical in the eye, blood inside the eye, or a sudden change in vision.
! No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, do the steps below first, then walk them out. Both eyes covered: someone leads them by the hand.
# Grit in the eye
1. Don't rub. Blink; tears often clear it.
2. Rinse with a gentle stream of clean water, away from the other eye.
3. Still there: with clean hands, pull the upper lid down over the lower lashes and let go.
4. Won't come out: cover loosely and get care.
# Chemical in the eye
1. Flush at once with lots of clean water (drinking water is fine) for at least 15 minutes, lids held open, inner corner outward, injured eye down and to the side. Keep flushing while you call.
2. Contact lenses that didn't wash out: take them out once the flush is done. Don't stop flushing to do it.
3. Get help now, even if it feels fine. Poison Control can say what the chemical does: see Poisoning and carbon monoxide.
# Object stuck (embedded) in the eye
1. Don't pull it out. Don't press. Don't rinse. Same for a cut eyeball.
2. Guard it with a cup taped over the eye, touching nothing.
3. Cover the other eye too, loosely: the eyes move together.
4. Sit them up, keep them still, get help now.
# Blow to the eye
1. Cool wet cloth, no pressure.
2. Double vision, blood inside, a pupil no longer round, or can't look all ways: get help now. Dazed: see Head injury.

# Snow blindness
- UV sunburn of the eye from snow, water, sand or altitude, even under cloud. Pain, grit feeling and light hurting start 6 to 12 hours later.
- Into a tent or dark place. Contacts out. Cool wet cloth over closed eyes. Acetaminophen or ibuprofen. Don't rub.
- Heals in 1 to 3 days. Not better by day 2: get care.
- Prevent it: UV-rated sunglasses or goggles with side shields, every day on snow. Lost them: tape or cardboard with narrow slits.
# Get care within a day for
- Pain that lasts, a red eye, light hurting, or any change in vision.
- Contact lens wearer with a sore red eye: lens out, leave it out, get care.
# Don't
- No rubbing, no swabs on the colored part, no drops or ointment unless a clinician says so.
- Don't try to neutralise a chemical. Water only.
