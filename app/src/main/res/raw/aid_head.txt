title: Head injury
keywords: concussion, skull, fall, hit head, knocked out, unconscious, neck, spine, helmet, vomiting, pupils
source: MedlinePlus (NIH), "Head injury: first aid" and "Concussion in adults - discharge"; CDC, "HEADS UP: Signs and Symptoms of Concussion"; CDC, "Preventing Traumatic Brain Injury in Older Adults"; Wilderness Medical Society, "Clinical Practice Guidelines for Spinal Cord Protection" (2024); American Heart Association, "Adult Basic Life Support" guidelines (2025); NOLS Wilderness Medicine, head injury evacuation guidance.
---
! Call 911 or your local emergency number if they:
- Were knocked out, even briefly
- Are confused, very drowsy, or can't be woken
- Have a worsening headache, or vomit more than once
- Have one pupil larger than the other, slurred speech, weakness or numbness
- Have a seizure, or clear fluid or blood from the nose or ears
No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone out for help.
# Do this
1. Keep the head and neck still and in line with the body. Assume the neck is hurt too.
2. Check breathing. Not breathing normally: start CPR (see Hands-only CPR). Tilt the head to open the airway if you must: breathing comes before the neck.
3. If they vomit, roll head, neck and body together onto their side.
4. Scalp bleeding: press firmly with a clean cloth. Skull may be broken: cover it, don't press. Leave anything stuck in it.
+++
# Don't
- Don't move them unless the spot is dangerous, cold or wet. Then move head, neck and body as one, onto a pad or jacket.
- Don't take off a helmet if the injury may be serious, unless it blocks breathing or CPR.
- Don't shake someone who seems dazed.
# After a mild knock
- On blood thinners: see a doctor now, even with no signs.
- Someone stays with them for the next day. Any sign above, even hours later: get help now.
- Rest that day; no alcohol for 2 days. Headache: acetaminophen (Tylenol), not aspirin, ibuprofen or naproxen.
- Not clearly better in a day: walk out together to a doctor.
