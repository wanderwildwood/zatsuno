! Bleeding that firm pressure won't stop: Call 911 or your local emergency number. See Severe bleeding.
# Do this
1. Get away from the animal. Don't try to catch or kill it.
2. Press on the wound with a clean cloth until the bleeding stops.
3. Wash the bite or scratch with soap and plenty of running water for 15 minutes, even if the skin barely broke. Flush deep bites hard with drinking water from a squeeze bottle. Then flush it with povidone-iodine if you carry it; it kills the rabies virus.
4. Cover it with a clean, dry dressing. Don't tape or glue it shut.
5. Note the animal: what kind, where it went, who owns it, whether its rabies shots are current. A photo from a distance helps.
6. Head out for medical care today. Rabies shots work only before symptoms start; don't wait for symptoms. Days from a road: still go. Shots started late still work as long as there are no symptoms.
! Any touch from a bat, or a bat found in a room or tent with someone who was asleep, a child, or anyone who can't say what happened: treat it as a bite even with no mark. Bat bites are tiny and easy to miss. Get care today.
+++
# Rabies risk
- High: bats, raccoons, foxes, skunks, coyotes, and any wild animal acting oddly, sick, or out in daytime.
- Low: squirrels, mice, rats, chipmunks, rabbits. Woodchucks count as high. Still wash and get the bite checked.
- Dogs, cats, ferrets: a healthy animal that can be found and watched for 10 days rarely means shots. Tell the clinic everything you know about it.
- Don't handle a bat bare-handed, even a dead one. Most US rabies deaths come from bats.
- Rabies shots started soon after a bite are nearly 100% effective. Once symptoms begin, rabies is almost always fatal.
# Also
- Tetanus: last shot over 5 years ago, or unknown, ask for one.
- Cat bites, hand and face bites, and deep punctures get infected easily; a doctor may give antibiotics.
- Infection over the next days: spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, a red streak, fever. Head out now. See Wound care.
- No hydrogen peroxide or alcohol in the wound. Soap and water is what matters.
> Report wild-animal bites and bat contact to the local health department; they decide whether the animal is tested.
