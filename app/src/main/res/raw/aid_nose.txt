! Call 911 or your local emergency number if the nose bleeds after a head injury (see Head injury), won't stop after 20 minutes of steady pinching or leaves them faint, or a jaw injury makes breathing hard.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, keep pinching and head out. After a head injury, don't walk them out: see Head injury.
# Nosebleed
1. Sit them up, leaning forward. Do not tilt the head back.
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose, below the bone. Hold 10 to 15 minutes by the clock. Don't let go to look.
3. Still bleeding: a decongestant nose spray (Afrin) in that nostril if you carry one (not for a child under 6), then pinch 10 minutes more.
# Knocked-out adult tooth
1. Pick it up by the crown, the chewing end. Don't touch the root.
2. Dirty: rinse a few seconds in milk or their own spit (water only if that's all you have). Don't scrub or dry it.
3. Push it straight back into its socket and bite gently on cloth.
4. Can't: keep it wet in milk or their own saliva (inside the cheek or in a cup). Never in water or a dry cloth.
5. Back in or not, a dentist as soon as you can reach one: it needs splinting and a tetanus check. Dry over an hour the odds are poor, but bring it anyway.
+++
# Chipped tooth, lost filling
- Rinse with warm water; save the pieces in milk. Cover a sharp edge with sugarless gum or wax.
- Dentist as soon as you can, the same day if it hurts, is loose, or a pink or bleeding spot shows in the break.
# Jaw
- Teeth that don't meet, a jaw that won't close, drooling: likely broken or dislocated. Don't push it back.
- Support it with a hand or a bandage under the chin and over the head, easy to pull off if they vomit. Hospital; heavy bleeding or a fast-swelling face: 911.
# Notes
- On blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban, clopidogrel): a doctor now, even if it stops.
- After a nosebleed: no blowing or sniffing for several hours. Don't pack the nose.
- Nose bent or flattened after a blow: likely broken. Cold pack, don't straighten it; walk out to a doctor.
- Baby teeth are not put back. Mouth cuts: press with gauze. See Wound care.
