title: Shock
keywords: pale, clammy, cold sweat, weak pulse, fast breathing, faint, confused, blood loss, low blood pressure
source: MedlinePlus (NIH), "Shock" and "Anaphylaxis"; Resuscitation Council UK, anaphylaxis guideline (2021).
---
! Call 911 or your local emergency number at the first signs of shock. Don't wait.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone now with your location.
# Signs
- Pale or gray, cool, clammy skin
- Fast, weak pulse; fast, shallow breathing
- Anxious, confused, drowsy or faint
- Thirst, nausea, vomiting
# Do this
1. Stop bleeding first (see Severe bleeding).
  Severe allergic reaction: use their epinephrine now.
2. Lay them flat. Pregnant: on the left side.
  Struggling to breathe: let them sit up, legs straight.
3. No head, neck, back or leg injury: raise the feet 12 inches (30 cm), unless it hurts.
4. Keep them warm, with something under them on cold ground. Loosen tight clothing.
5. Stay with them and keep checking breathing. Not breathing normally: start CPR (see Hands-only CPR).
# Don't
! Don't let them stand or walk out, even if they feel better. Bring help to them.
+++
- Nothing to eat or drink if drowsy, vomiting or badly hurt. Fully awake and cold, overheated or dried out: see Hypothermia, Heat exhaustion and heat stroke, or Dehydration.
- Possible spine injury: move them only if the spot is dangerous, cold or wet. Then move head, neck and body together.
- Vomiting: roll them onto their side in one piece.
