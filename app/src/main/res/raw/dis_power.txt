! Headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, chest pain or confusion while a generator, grill or heater runs: get everyone into fresh air and call your local emergency number (112 or 911). See Poisoning and carbon monoxide.
# Generators and heat
1. A generator runs outside only, 20 feet (6 m) or more from any window, door or vent. Never in the house, basement or garage, even with doors open. Use an extension cord long enough.
2. Use a battery or battery-backup carbon monoxide alarm.
3. Never use a grill, hibachi, camp stove or lantern inside a home, tent or camper. Never heat a home with a gas range or oven. Never leave a car running in a garage.
4. Too hot or too cold at home: go to friends or a community shelter. Fans don't prevent heat illness.
# Food and medicine
- Keep the fridge and freezer shut. A shut fridge keeps food safe 4 hours; a full freezer 48 hours, half full 24.
- Throw out meat, poultry, fish, eggs and leftovers from a fridge off 4 hours or more, and freezer food that has thawed. Food still with ice crystals, or as cold as a fridge, can be refrozen or cooked.
- Medicine that needs the fridge: power off a day or more, throw it out unless the label says otherwise. If a life depends on it, keep using it until you get new, then replace it soon.
+++
# Don't
- Don't touch a fallen power line. Don't drive through water with a downed line in it.
- Use flashlights, not candles. A candle: keep it away from anything that burns, and stay near it.
- Don't siphon gasoline. Swallowed or breathed, it harms the lungs.
- Sleeping or drinking people can die of carbon monoxide before they feel anything.
- Water may not be safe when the power is out. Use bottled, boiled or treated water; see Water disinfection.
