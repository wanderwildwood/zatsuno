---
! Any sudden sign below may be a stroke. Call 911 or your local emergency number now, even if it fades.
  No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send two people for help now if you can, with the start time and signs written down. The rest stay with them.
# BE FAST
- Balance: sudden dizziness or stumbling.
- Eyes: sudden blurred, double or lost vision.
- Face: ask them to smile. Does one side droop?
- Arms: raise both. Does one drift down?
- Speech: say a short phrase. Slurred, wrong words, or none?
- Time: call at once. One sign is enough.
- Also: sudden numbness or weakness of a leg, especially on one side; sudden confusion; or the worst headache of their life.
# Do
1. Write down when it started, or when they were last seen normal.
2. Nothing to eat or drink, not even aspirin. Swallowing may be weak.
3. Rest them lying down, head and shoulders a little raised. Keep them warm and calm.
4. Drowsy, vomiting or drooling: roll them onto their side. See Scene size-up and primary assessment.
5. Not breathing normally: start CPR. See Hands-only CPR.
- Diabetes: low blood sugar can look like a stroke. Use their meter if they carry one. Low, or no meter, and they can sit up and swallow normally: give sugar. See Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia).
+++
# Getting out
- It can't be treated out here. Speed to a hospital is everything.
- Don't let them walk or drive. Bring help to them, or carry or drive them out if no help can come.
- Signs that come and go still need a hospital now. A bigger stroke may follow.
