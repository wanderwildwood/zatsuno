title: Hypothermia
keywords: cold, shivering, wet, exposure, hypothermia, confusion, slurred speech, fumbling, winter, cold water
source: CDC, "Preventing Hypothermia" and "Avoid, Spot, Treat Frostbite and Hypothermia"; Wilderness Medical Society, Accidental Hypothermia Guidelines (2019); NIH MedlinePlus, "Hypothermia".
---
! Can happen above 40°F (4°C) when wet. Confused, drowsy, or shivering stops: emergency. Call 911 or your local emergency number. No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone for help.
# Signs
- Shivering, tiredness, fumbling hands
- Worse: confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness, shivering stops
# Do this
1. Out of wind and wet. Insulate them from the ground.
2. In shelter, remove wet clothes. No shelter: leave them on, wrap plastic or foil over.
3. Warm chest, neck, head and groin first: dry layers, sleeping bag, skin-to-skin. Wrap heat packs and hot bottles in cloth. Never direct heat or hot water on skin.
4. Alert, shivering and can swallow: warm, sweet drinks or food. No alcohol.
5. Handle gently. Don't rub arms or legs. Keep them sitting or lying down.
- Breathing can be very slow: check for a full minute. Not breathing normally: start CPR (see Hands-only CPR), keep warming. A very cold person can look dead and still be saved.
+++
- Alert and shivering: once warm, fed and steady, they can walk out slowly.
- Confused, drowsy or not shivering: keep lying flat and warming. Don't let them walk.
