title: Seizures
keywords: seizure, fit, convulsions, epilepsy, shaking, jerking, twitching, collapsed, blank stare, foaming, unresponsive, bit tongue
source: CDC, "First Aid for Seizures"; MedlinePlus (NIH), "Seizures" and "Seizure first aid"; Epilepsy Foundation, "Seizure First Aid"; AHA and American Red Cross, "2024 Guidelines for First Aid".
---
! Call 911 or your local emergency number if:
- It lasts over 5 minutes, or another starts soon after (even if they woke up)
- It is their first seizure, or you don't know
- It happened in water
- They are pregnant, diabetic, hurt, or have a head injury
- They struggle to breathe, or don't wake up after
No signal: try 911 anyway (see Calling for help); if it fails, send someone out for help.
# During the seizure
1. Note the time.
2. Ease them to the ground. Move rocks, gear and people away.
3. Put something soft under the head. Take off glasses. Loosen anything at the neck.
4. If you can do it gently, turn them onto their side. Don't force it.
5. In water: if you can reach them safely, hold the face above water, then get them out once it stops. See Drowning (submersion).
# After it stops
1. Roll them onto their side, mouth toward the ground.
2. Not breathing, or only gasping: call 911 or your local emergency number and start CPR. See Hands-only CPR.
- Heavy, noisy or snoring breaths right after a seizure are common. Keep them on their side and watch.
3. Stay with them. Confusion and sleepiness are normal.
4. Protect them from the ground and weather: a pad or pack under them. A layer over them if cold; shade, not layers, if hot.
+++
# Don't
- Don't hold them down or stop the movements.
- Don't put anything in the mouth. They can't swallow their tongue.
- No food or water until fully awake.
# Think about why
- Hot and confused: see Heat exhaustion and heat stroke.
- Diabetic: once fully awake and able to swallow, give sugar. See Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia).
- Drank lots of plain water on a long, hot day: no more water. See Dehydration.
- High up a mountain: go down. See Altitude illness (AMS, HACE, HAPE).
- Recent fall or blow: see Head injury.
- No help coming: once fully back to normal, walk out together, away from edges and water.
