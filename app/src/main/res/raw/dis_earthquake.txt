title: Earthquake
keywords: earthquake, quake, shaking, tremor, aftershock, drop cover hold on, tsunami, doorway, triangle of life
source: Earthquake Country Alliance, "Step 5: Drop, Cover, and Hold On" (with FEMA, USGS and state emergency agencies).
---
! Shaking: Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Don't run outside.
# Now
1. Drop where you are onto your hands and knees, before it knocks you down.
2. Cover your head and neck with one arm and hand. A sturdy table or desk within a few steps: get under it. Don't try to move more than 5 to 7 feet (2 m).
3. Hold on until the shaking stops. Under a table, hold it with one hand and move with it. No shelter: hold your head and neck with both arms.
# Where you are
- In bed: stay there. Lie face down and cover your head and neck with a pillow.
- Can't get to the floor: bend forward, cover your head with your arms and hold your neck.
- Wheelchair or walker: lock the wheels, get as low as you can, cover your head and neck with your arms, a book or a pillow, and hold on.
- Outdoors: move to open ground if you can do it safely, away from power lines, trees, signs and buildings. Then drop, cover and hold on.
- Driving: pull over, stop and set the parking brake, away from overpasses, bridges, power lines, signs and trees. Stay in the car until the shaking stops. A power line on the car: stay inside until a trained person moves it.
- Tall building: no elevators. Sprinklers and fire alarms may go off.
! By the sea: once you can stand, walk fast to high ground or inland. A tsunami can come within minutes. Don't wait for a warning, and walk rather than drive.
+++
# After
- Expect aftershocks. Watch for anything that could fall as you leave.
- Driving on: look out for debris, cracked or shifted road and emergency vehicles.
# Don't
- Don't run outside during the shaking. Glass and the outsides of buildings fall as people leave.
- Don't stand in a doorway. It is no stronger than the rest of the house and doesn't shield you from falling things.
> Standing in a doorway was once taught, and the "triangle of life" (lying beside furniture, not under it) is still passed around. Earthquake experts call both out of date or dangerous; Drop, Cover, and Hold On is the advice.
